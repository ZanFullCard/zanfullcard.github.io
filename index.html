The California Housing Analysis

We used 1990 California census data to predict median house value and compared four models: a mean baseline, simple and multiple linear regression, and kNN regression. kNN performed best on validation data, explaining about 71% of the variation in house values, and median income was the strongest predictor.

Completed August 2026. Team project with 2 other team members.

Predicting and Reducing Patient No-Shows

A project using a Central Ohio dental practice’s appointment data to flag appointments at risk of becoming no-shows and to support interventions that could bring the current 14.1% no-show rate below 10%.

Expected completion December 2026.

Capstone story structure
  1. The problem: missed appointments waste clinic time and result in a loss of revenue. The practice would like the no-show rate to drop below 10%.
  2. The question: how can the dental practice use appointment scheduling patterns, patient attendance history, and confirmation behavior to identify appointments at risk of becoming no-shows and support interventions intended to reduce the no-show rate from its current baseline of 14.1% to below 10%?
  3. The data: a patient and appointment dataset from a Central Ohio dental practice.
  4. The analysis: calculate the patterns behind no-shows, such as scheduling lead time, attendance history, and confirmation behavior, followed by 3+ visualizations and 1 comprehensive interactive dashboard.
  5. The findings: what drives missed visits and how well the model works.
  6. The recommendations: possible changes such as sending more or fewer reminders and shortening/lengthening the lead time when booking.
  7. Limitations and next steps: the data comes from one dental office in a multi-location practice, so results may not apply to every location.
Planned artifacts
  • Tableau dashboard: an interactive view of appointment outcome distribution, short-notice break rate by day of the week, short-notice break rate by confirmation status, and more.
  • Google Colab notebook: a Python comparison of models, including logistic regression, with a confusion matrix. All of my code will be done in Google Colab, using the pandas and seaborn libraries to clean, explore, and model the dataset.
